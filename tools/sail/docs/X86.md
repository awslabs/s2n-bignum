# x86

Two external Sail models supply x86 instruction bodies: shina-lab's
sail-x86 ("shina", [x86_shina.ml](../backend/x86_shina.ml)) and REMS's
sail-x86-from-acl2 ("REMS", [x86_rems.ml](../backend/x86_rems.ml)), a Sail
translation of the ACL2 X86isa model. [x86.ml](../backend/x86.ml) holds the
form names, widths and register fields they share. Each compiler run reads
one model, and the arithmetic and flag calculations stay in that model's
bodies. Each model is compared with s2n-bignum's relations in
`x86/proofs/x86.ml`, not with the other model. The interpretations used
for x86, with what each loses, are in
[TRUST.md](../TRUST.md#interpretations); the differences found are in
[X86-DIVERGENCES.md](X86-DIVERGENCES.md).

## Scope

[x86_forms.py](../scripts/x86_forms.py) lists the required forms:
register forms of scalar s2n-bignum definitions (`GPR`; IMUL has a
one-operand and a two-operand definition) and of AVX2 integer
instructions (`VECTOR`). Scalar forms have widths 8, 16, 32 and 64,
except ADCX, ADOX and MULX (32, 64) and two-operand IMUL, SHLD and SHRD
(16, 32, 64). At 8 bits there are two forms: with REX, whose 4-bit fields
select the low byte of all sixteen registers, and `_LEGACY`, without REX,
whose 3-bit fields select AL to BL and AH to BH. Vector forms are at 128
and 256 bits. The README's [scope table](../README.md#what-is-proved)
gives the numbers.

Not covered: memory operands, instruction decoding, counts read from CL,
and every s2n-bignum x86 definition outside the list.

Notes on the statements:

- A legacy byte register is
  `if 4 <= val d then GPR8 (Gpr (iword (&(val d) - &4)) Upper_8)
  else GPR8 (Gpr (word_zx d) Lower_8)`.
- 32-bit operands are `GPR32_Z`, whose writes clear the upper half, also
  for the SHLD and SHRD source.
- Shift and rotate forms take an arbitrary count byte and compare with
  s2n-bignum at the masked count, `val count MOD 64` for 64-bit operands
  and `val count MOD 32` otherwise.
- One-operand IMUL (`X86_IMUL1...`) compares with `x86_IMUL2`, which
  writes RDX:RAX; two-operand IMUL compares with `x86_IMUL3 D (D,M)`.
  MULX keeps the implicit RDX source and independent high and low
  destinations.

## shina

shina's bodies take resolved operands. The ALU entries call
`exec_alu_reg_rm` with the register alternative `RM_reg` of a
register-or-memory operand; the upstream helpers keep their slicing,
high-byte selection and upper-half clearing. Only their accesses to the
sixteen-element `GPR` array and the six one-bit flag registers are
interpreted. The roots do not change RIP, memory or events.

A few entries keep more of the upstream decoder: truncated IMUL, MULX,
ADCX, ADOX and TEST go through the selected opcode branch and its
`fetch_modrm`, with only the fetched bytes supplied; the vector entries
supply the ModR/M byte, and for immediate shifts the count, to the
upstream VEX bodies, which still decode them. VPTEST is selected by
fixing `op = 23` in `dispatch_vex_0f38_arith`; its body still checks the
mandatory prefix and the reserved VEX field. Fetch faults, `decode_pos`
updates, prefix decoding before the selected body and the enclosing AVX
availability checks are outside the comparison.

## REMS

REMS's instruction bodies take the decoded instruction as arguments:
processor mode, start and current instruction pointer, the prefix
record, the REX byte, the opcode and the ModR/M and SIB bytes. Each entry
calls one body with a register-direct encoding. ADD64, for example, is

```text
x86_add_adc_sub_sbb_or_and_xor_cmp_g_e(0, Mode_64bit, 0, 3,
  Mk_prefixes(...), 0b0100 @ 0b1 @ d[3..3] @ 0b0 @ m[3..3],
  0x03, Mk_modr_m(0b11 @ d[2..0] @ m[2..0]), Mk_sib(0x00))
```

that is `ADD r64, r/m64` (REX.W 03 /r) with destination `d` and source
`m`. The body keeps its operand decoding, arithmetic, flags and RIP
update. The synthetic span from 0 to the end of the ModR/M byte passes
the body's length and canonical-address checks; it does not fix the HOL
RIP. The shift count byte replaces the body's immediate fetch.

REMS has no body for the vector forms or for ADCX, ADOX and MULX at its
pinned commit. Every branch of its VEX dispatch
(`model/vex_opcodes_dispatch.sail`) ends in `x86_model_error("Opcode
Unimplemented in x86isa!")` or a fault, and its `ext_vex_*` hooks return
false; MULX is VEX opcode 0F 38 F6. ADCX and ADOX are in the non-VEX
0F 38 map, whose escape throws "Translation error: two-byte opcode 56
not translated" (`model/two_byte_opcodes_dispatch.sail`). These forms
are compared for shina only.

REMS bodies reach Sail constructs that the Arm, RV32 and shina exports do
not. The REMS profile (`extended_evaluation` and the
`Uncaught_exceptions` binding in [x86_rems.ml](../backend/x86_rems.ml))
turns on these translator rules, each part of the trusted translation:

- A throw (`x86_fault`, `x86_model_error`) permits no final state; the
  translator supports no `try`. Since s2n-bignum's relations always have
  a final state, an `_EQ` theorem shows every throw unreachable for the
  inputs it covers.
- A shift by an amount that the translator cannot prove nonnegative
  becomes `if &0 <= amount then <shift> else u`, with `u` an unknown word
  (`Sail-negative-shift-unknown`). REMS's 8- and 16-bit rotates shift by
  `width - count`, which is negative for counts above the rotated width.
- An `undefined` value of a refined integer type, such as `nat`, is a
  choice within the refinement; a choice outside it permits no final state
  (`Sail-undefined-refined-integer`).
- Direct recursion on integer values, such as `logcount_int`, is
  unfolded while one argument shrinks and none grows, up to fixed limits.
  Each unfolded call is the original body.
- The profile also decides more conditions statically and supports
  matches on Booleans, guarded variables and short-circuit values with
  one continuation. These translate the same relation; the other sources
  keep the earlier translation so that their models stay unchanged.

REMS's GPR accessors `read_gpr` and `write_gpr` are cuts. Before cutting
them the translator checks that they use the x86 register numbering (0
RAX, 1 RCX, ..., 15 R15) and that `write_gpr` does nothing but its update
and `log_gpr_write`. REMS's 32-bit `rflags` and its `rip` are root
parameters, which the statements instantiate as `rflags :> bottom_32` and
`RIP`. Each flag in REMS's undefined-flag mask gets an independent
unknown value.

REMS bodies end by writing the next instruction address to RIP; here
that is the end of the synthetic span, a constant. s2n-bignum's relations
leave RIP unchanged. The statements therefore compare

```text
sail_hl_x86_core R s t <=> ?u. R s u /\ t = write RIP (read RIP s) u
```

which resets RIP to its entry value and observes every other component.
REMS roots, types and constructors carry the output stem in their names
(`sail_hl_X86_5fREMS_5f...`, `..._x86_2drems_2d...`), so both models can
load into one kernel.

## What the proofs show

For shina, every arithmetic and vector form agrees with s2n-bignum,
including all register aliases, every flag and the rest of the state.
Every shift and rotate agrees except RCL and RCR at 8 and 16 bits. For
those, an `_EXACT` theorem states shina's relation exactly: s2n-bignum's
relation with OF also kept at whole-cycle counts. So shina permits a
subset of what s2n-bignum permits, and a `_DIFF` theorem shows that the
inclusion is strict.

For REMS, every arithmetic form agrees. Among the shifts and rotates, SHL
and SHR at every width, SAR, RCL and RCR at 32 and 64 bits, and SHLD and
SHRD at every width agree. The other REMS forms (ROL and ROR at every
width; SAR, RCL and RCR at 8 and 16 bits) have `_DIFF` theorems, which
show that at least these differences exist:

- ROR's carry flag;
- 8- and 16-bit SAR's carry flag at counts of at least the width;
- ROL's and ROR's OF at masked count zero;
- 8- and 16-bit rotates at counts above the rotated width, where the
  negative-shift rule above gives an unknown result.

No exact relation is proved for these forms.
[X86-DIVERGENCES.md](X86-DIVERGENCES.md) gives each difference, its
witness and which relation permits more.

## Shift corrections

s2n-bignum's narrow shift relations used to fix values that the
architecture leaves undefined. The comparison with shina showed this,
and two x86 model commits at the start of this series correct it;
awslabs/s2n-bignum#484 contains the same fixes. The corrected relations
keep the undefined choices and preserve every unaffected state
component.

- 8- and 16-bit SHL and SHR: Sail's `exec_shl` and `exec_shr` make CF
  undefined when the count is at least the operand width
  (`model/insn_groups.sail:297,334`). The old `x86_SHL` and `x86_SHR`
  always set CF to the last bit shifted out; for an 8-bit zero operand and
  count 8 that fixed CF to false. The masked counts concerned are 8 to 31
  for bytes and 16 to 31 for 16-bit operands; 32- and 64-bit counts are
  masked below the width.
- 16-bit SHLD and SHRD: for counts above the width, Sail's `exec_shld`
  and `exec_shrd` make the result and all six flags undefined
  (`model/insn_baseline.sail:365-373,424-432`). The old relations fixed
  the result and CF, SF, ZF and PF. With AX = BX = 0, `SHLD AX,BX,17` now
  permits AX = 1 as well as 0.

Intel's Software Developer's Manual, Volume 2B (order 253667-093US),
specifies undefined values in both cases (pages 4-606 and 4-640 to
4-644). The proofs in [x86-shift.ml](../proofs/x86-shift.ml) cover every
count byte for these forms. Neither correction changes decodable
behaviour of the library: no 8- or 16-bit SHL or SHR occurs in it, and
the decoder does not accept 16-bit SHLD or SHRD.
