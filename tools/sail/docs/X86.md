# x86

Two external Sail models supply x86 instruction bodies: shina-lab's
sail-x86 ("shina", [x86_shina.ml](../backend/x86_shina.ml)) and REMS's
sail-x86-from-acl2 ("REMS", [x86_rems.ml](../backend/x86_rems.ml)), a Sail
translation of the ACL2 X86isa model. [x86.ml](../backend/x86.ml) holds the
form names, widths and register fields they share. Each compiler run reads
one model, and the arithmetic and flag calculations stay in that model's
bodies. Each model is compared with s2n-bignum's relations in
`x86/proofs/x86.ml`, not with the other model. The interpretations used
for x86, with what each loses, are in
[interpretations](../backend/README.md#interpretations); the differences
found are in [X86-DIVERGENCES.md](X86-DIVERGENCES.md).

## Scope

[x86_forms.py](../scripts/x86_forms.py) lists the required forms:
register forms of scalar s2n-bignum definitions (`GPR`; IMUL has a
one-operand and a two-operand definition) and of AVX2 integer
instructions (`VECTOR`), and register forms of 30 further AVX2
instructions for data movement, shuffles, compares, blends, inserts, extracts,
mask extracts and carry-less multiplication (`AVX2`; this includes the
legacy-encoded PMOVMSKB). The suite `x86-sse` adds register forms of 22
legacy-encoded SSE integer instructions, VZEROUPPER, VPEXTRD and VPEXTRQ
(`SSE`), and five AES-NI instructions (`AES`). The `x86-stack` suite
adds LEAVE64 with its implicit eight-byte load under the declared total
flat-memory interpretation, rather than a register-only form.
The REMS comparison is limited to the original 205 `GPR` and `VECTOR`
forms. It exports 119 of them. The later shina-only forms are outside
that comparison: the catalogue makes no claim about whether their
instruction bodies exist in REMS.
Scalar forms have widths 8, 16, 32 and 64, except ADCX, ADOX
and MULX (32, 64) and two-operand IMUL, SHLD and SHRD (16, 32, 64). At 8
bits there are two forms: with REX, whose 4-bit fields select the low
byte of all sixteen registers, and `_LEGACY`, without REX, whose 3-bit
fields select AL to BL and AH to BH. Vector forms are at 128 and 256
bits. The README's [scope table](../README.md#what-is-proved) gives the
numbers.

Twenty-five further scalar definitions have register forms in the suites
`x86-move`, `x86-select`, `x86-bit`, `x86-count` and `x86-pext` (`MOVE`,
`SELECT`, `BIT`, `COUNT`, `PEXT` in x86_forms.py). MOVSXD is another
encoding of `x86_MOVSX`, not another definition. Two-operand IMUL also
has a second theorem against `x86_IMUL`:

- `x86-move`: CLC, STC, CMC, CLD, STD, NOP, ENDBR64, NOP_N, and MOV and
  XCHG at 8 (REX and legacy), 16, 32 and 64 bits. The shina roots select
  the opcode branch of `decode_execute` (or, for ENDBR64 and NOP_N, of
  `dispatch_2byte_gpr`) with the prefix record supplied, so
  `scan_prefixes` is outside the comparison as for TEST. CLC, STC, CMC,
  CLD, STD and NOP fetch nothing after the opcode and take no input.
  ENDBR64 is the byte sequence F3 0F 1E FA, whose branch is the one that
  Sail shares between the reserved NOP opcodes 0F 18 to 0F 1E;
  NOP_N is 0F 1F with a register-direct operand. NOP, ENDBR64 and NOP_N
  leave the whole state unchanged, as in s2n-bignum.
- `x86-select`: CMOVcc at 32 and 64 bits and SETcc on a byte register
  (REX or legacy), one root per condition code, because the condition is
  part of the fixed opcode; MOVZX and MOVSX from a byte (REX or legacy)
  or word register to a 32- or 64-bit register; BSWAP at 32 and 64 bits,
  one root per register number in the opcode, with REX.B a one-bit
  parameter. The widths are those that s2n-bignum's decoder produces
  (it rejects the operand-size prefix for these), so there is no 16-bit
  CMOV or BSWAP. MOVSXD (63 /r, a 32-bit register to a 64-bit one, or
  to a 32-bit one without REX.W) is compared with `x86_MOVSX`, as
  s2n-bignum's decoder produces. Its branch in `decode_execute` shares the
  opcode with ARPL and calls `fetch_modrm` once per processor mode; the
  entry replaces the LongMode call with a supplied input
  (`Entry.input_lift ~selected_call:0`) and keeps the others as original
  code, which the assumption `cur_mode = LongMode` makes unreachable.
- `x86-bit`: BT, BTS, BTR and BTC on 32- and 64-bit registers, with the
  offset in a register. Shina leaves ZF unchanged where s2n-bignum
  leaves it undefined
  ([X86-DIVERGENCES.md](X86-DIVERGENCES.md#bt-bts-btr-and-btc-leave-zf-unchanged)).
- `x86-count`: BSF, BSR, TZCNT, LZCNT and POPCNT on 16-, 32- and 64-bit
  registers (`has_bmi1`, `has_lzcnt` and `has_popcnt` are assumed true,
  and the F3 prefix selects TZCNT, LZCNT or POPCNT). Sail computes them
  with branchless helpers and asserts that the popcount is at most the
  width. The translator turns such an assertion into a guard that rejects
  the final state; the proofs show that the guard always holds, by proving
  that the helpers compute `word_popcount`, `word_ctz` and `word_clz`. TZCNT
  and LZCNT agree with s2n-bignum. POPCNT agrees after the event
  `EventX86POPCNT(x, width)` that s2n-bignum appends: Sail has no events,
  so the statement follows Sail's relation by that event, taken from
  s2n-bignum and not checked, as for the RV32 division events
  ([backend/README.md](../backend/README.md#interpretations)). BSF
  differs for a zero source: shina keeps the destination, s2n-bignum makes
  it undefined
  ([X86-DIVERGENCES.md](X86-DIVERGENCES.md#bsf-and-bsr-keep-the-destination-for-a-zero-source)),
  and so does BSR, which takes `63 - clz(zero_extend(x) | 1)` with the
  helper of LZCNT; for a nonzero source BSR agrees. The generated BSR body
  is a chain of 38 lets in which each value is used twice, and the
  popcount that ends the chain occurs three times once the next lets are
  substituted (in the first bound check and, through `64 - count`, in the
  second check and in the index). Substituting the lets copies the operand
  2^12 times for each occurrence and
  gives a goal of 660,000 nodes, whose rewriting overflows the 8 MiB stack
  of the compiled proof drivers. The proof therefore first rewrites the
  chain, before any let is substituted, with
  `SAIL_HL_X86_BSR_CHAIN` ([x86-count-lemmas.ml](../proofs/x86-count-lemmas.ml)):
  for any continuation, the chain passes it `word(64 - word_clz y)` for
  `y = zero_extend(x) | 1`. That lemma is proved once on a variable. The
  normalized goal is then 40,000 nodes and the suite needs no more than
  the default stack.

Not covered: memory operands, the immediate forms of MOV, BT, BTS, BTR
and BTC, instruction decoding, counts read from CL, and every s2n-bignum x86 definition outside the lists. Among the AVX2
data-movement instructions, some forms have no entry or theorem, either
because the translator cannot yet translate their Sail bodies or because
the proof is not done; [AVX2 data movement](#avx2-data-movement) says why.

PEXT (VEX F3 0F38 F5, BMI2) is in its own suite, `x86-pext`, with one
`_EQ` theorem at 32 and one at 64 bits (`has_bmi2` is assumed true and the
F3 prefix selects PEXT in the dispatcher it shares with BZHI and PDEP; as
for MULX the prefix record sets both `vex_w` and `rex_w`). Sail's body is
a loop of 32 or 64 steps. Step `i` stores `src[i] & mask[i]` at position
`k` of a temporary and adds `mask[i]` to `k`, which is a one-bit write at
a mutable local that Sail's type checker bounds by the word width, so the
body translates as it is. s2n-bignum's `x86_PEXT` is `word_condense` of the
operands and also appends `EventX86PEXT(x, y, width)` to the events;
Sail has no events, so the statement follows Sail's relation by that
event, taken from s2n-bignum and not checked, as for POPCNT.

The proof keeps the loop as an induction and does not normalize it flat.
A flat normalization of the 32 steps takes a few seconds and gives a
33,000-node term of nested index and temporary expressions, in which no
step can be recognized. Instead the proof folds each step, as it stands
in the generated body, into one application of a function
`sail_hl_x86_pext_step i x y` on the pair (`k`, temporary), and makes
sure that each application receives the previous pair once. This needs
care: if a step is passed `FST p` and `SND p` instead of `p`, the term
doubles at every step. [x86-pext-lemmas.ml](../proofs/x86-pext-lemmas.ml)
then proves, by induction on the number of steps `n` up to 64, that
after `n` steps, if `c` is the number of set mask bits below `n`, then
`k = c MOD 64` and the temporary is the sum that `word_condense`
takes over the first `n` bits, below `2 EXP c`. Before another step,
`c <= n < 64`, so `k = c` and the temporary's bit `k` is clear. The
bit insertion therefore adds `2^k` times the bit. With an all-ones
64-bit mask, the final `k` wraps to zero, but there is no further write
and the bound remains `2 EXP 64`, not `2 EXP k`. For 32 bits the operands
are zero-extended to 64 and the low 32 bits of the temporary are taken; the
sums agree term by term below bit 32.

Notes on the statements:

- A legacy byte register is
  `if 4 <= val d then GPR8 (Gpr (iword (&(val d) - &4)) Upper_8)
  else GPR8 (Gpr (word_zx d) Lower_8)`.
- 32-bit operands are `GPR32_Z`, whose writes clear the upper half, also
  for the SHLD and SHRD source.
- Shift and rotate forms take an arbitrary count byte and compare with
  s2n-bignum at the masked count, `val count MOD 64` for 64-bit operands
  and `val count MOD 32` otherwise.
- One-operand IMUL (`X86_IMUL1...`) compares with `x86_IMUL2`, which
  writes RDX:RAX; two-operand IMUL compares with `x86_IMUL3 D (D,M)`.
  MULX keeps the implicit RDX source and independent high and low
  destinations.

## shina

shina's bodies take resolved operands. The ALU entries call
`exec_alu_reg_rm` with the register alternative `RM_reg` of a
register-or-memory operand; the upstream helpers keep their slicing,
high-byte selection and upper-half clearing. Only their accesses to the
sixteen-element `GPR` array and the six one-bit flag registers are
interpreted. These ALU roots do not change RIP, memory or events.

A few entries keep more of the upstream decoder: truncated IMUL, MULX,
ADCX, ADOX and TEST go through the selected opcode branch and its
`fetch_modrm`, with only the fetched bytes supplied; the vector entries
supply the ModR/M byte, and for immediate shifts the count, to the
upstream VEX bodies, which still decode them. VPTEST is selected by
fixing `op = 23` in `dispatch_vex_0f38_arith`; its body still checks the
mandatory prefix and the reserved VEX field. Fetch faults, `decode_pos`
updates, prefix decoding before the selected body and the enclosing AVX
availability checks are outside the comparison.

### AVX2 data movement

The `x86-avx2` suite compares the register forms of VPSHUFB, VPUNPCKLQDQ,
VPUNPCKHQDQ, VPBROADCASTD/Q/W, VPBLENDD, VPBLENDW, VPBLENDVB, VPERMQ,
VPERMD, VPERM2I128, VINSERTI128, VEXTRACTI128, VMOVSLDUP, VMOVSHDUP,
VMOVDQA, VMOVDQU, VMOVD, VMOVQ, VPEXTRW, VPINSRW, VPINSRD, VPINSRQ,
VPCLMULQDQ, VPSHUFD, VPCMPEQW, VMOVMSKPS, VPMOVMSKB and PMOVMSKB.
Where an instruction
has a Sail helper that takes the decoded prefix (`vex_exec_binop`,
`vex_exec_unpack`, `vex_exec_broadcast`, `vex_exec_blend`,
`vex_exec_lane_bin_imm`), the entry calls that helper; otherwise it fixes
the opcode of the dispatcher (`dispatch_vex_0f_int`, `dispatch_vex_0f_fp`,
`dispatch_vex_0f3a`, `dispatch_vex_0f38_arith`, and for PMOVMSKB the
legacy `dispatch_2byte_sse_int`), as for VPTEST. The
ModR/M byte, and the imm8 where there is one, are supplied inputs. A
`_STORE` form is the encoding with the destination in ModR/M.r/m.
VPERMQ, VPERMD, VPERM2I128, VINSERTI128, VEXTRACTI128, VMOVMSKPS and
VPMOVMSKB exist at 256 bits only (s2n-bignum reads a 256-bit source for
the two mask extracts), VMOVD, VMOVQ, VPEXTRW, VPINSRW, VPINSRD and
VPINSRQ at 128 bits, and VPBLENDVB and VPCLMULQDQ at both. PMOVMSKB has a
32- and a 64-bit destination, as in s2n-bignum's decoder.
General-register operands are `GPR32_Z` or `GPR64`. VPSHUFD and
VPCMPEQW have both 128- and 256-bit forms. VPSHUFD uses each pair of
imm8 bits to select a doubleword within each 128-bit lane and reserves
decoded VEX.vvvv=0. VPCMPEQW compares each pair of 16-bit words and
writes all ones for equality or zero otherwise.

Three of these need a note on how the Sail body reaches the HOL statement.

- VPBLENDVB takes its mask register from imm8[7:4] (VEX opcode 0F3A 4C,
  "is4" encoding). The root takes the register number `k` as a parameter,
  supplies `k @ 0b0000` as imm8, and the theorem compares with
  s2n-bignum's fourth operand, the vector register `k`. s2n-bignum
  assembles the result as a sum of 8-bit values weighted by powers of two;
  the proof relates bit `k` of that sum to bit `k MOD 8` of byte
  `k DIV 8` once for 16 and 32 bytes.
- VPCLMULQDQ compares Sail's 64 unrolled steps
  `result ^ ((zero_extend(y) << i) & sign_extend(x[i]))` with
  `word_pmul`, by unrolling `WORD_PMUL_STEP` 64 times (as for Arm PMULL).
  Both operands' quadword choice (imm8[0] and imm8[4]) is compared as is.
- VPERM2I128 and VPINSRQ reach a match on `unsigned(...)` of a bit field
  of at most two bits (`vperm2_select`, the VPINSRQ lane choice). The
  translator expands such a match into one conditional per value
  ([backend README](../backend/README.md#what-the-translator-checks-or-refuses),
  "Integer matches").

Not translated or not proved:

- VPEXTRD and VPEXTRQ are in the `x86-sse` suite, because their 0F3A
  body reads `ZMM[src]` directly ([Legacy SSE](#legacy-sse)).
- VMOVHPD needs a memory operand.

### Legacy SSE

The `x86-sse` suite compares register forms of PADDD, PADDQ, PAND, PXOR,
PCMPGTW, PCMPGTD, PSHUFB, PSHUFD, PBLENDW, PCLMULQDQ, PINSRD, PINSRQ,
PSRLW, PSRAD, PSLLDQ, PSRLDQ,
MOVAPS, MOVUPS, MOVDQA, MOVDQU, MOVD and MOVQ, and VZEROUPPER, VPEXTRD
and VPEXTRQ, 32 forms in all. The entries are those of the AVX2 suite with a
legacy prefix record (66, F3 or none; REX.R and REX.B supply bit 3 of the
register numbers) in place of the VEX one: they fix the opcode of
`dispatch_2byte_sse_int`, `dispatch_2byte_sse_fp`,
`dispatch_3byte_38_sse_int`, `dispatch_3byte_3a_sse_int` or
`dispatch_vex_0f_int` (VZEROUPPER, `dispatch_vex_0f3a` for VPEXTRD and
VPEXTRQ), or for PADDD, PADDQ, PAND and PXOR reach `sse_exec_binop`
through the dispatcher. The ModR/M byte and imm8 are supplied inputs. A
`_STORE` form has the destination in ModR/M.r/m, `_F3` is MOVQ xmm,
xmm/m64. MOVQ's other store encoding (66 0F D6) has no entry: its Sail
branch has one `fetch` per mandatory prefix, and the supplied-input
derivation requires exactly one.

A legacy SSE body assigns the low 128 bits of a vector register and keeps
bits 128 to 511 (`ZMM[xreg][127..0] = ...`), where a VEX body, through
`write_simd`, clears them. The statements therefore use s2n-bignum's
`SIMD128_SSE` for the legacy forms and `SIMD128` for VPEXTRD and VPEXTRQ,
which are VEX-encoded and write a general register. The register file is
the raw `ZMM` bank, which the translator maps to `simdregisters`
([interpretations](../backend/README.md#interpretations)). Two lemmas in
[x86-sse-lemmas.ml](../proofs/x86-sse-lemmas.ml) relate `SIMD128_SSE`
(`bottom_256 :> bottom_128`) to the two things Sail's body does: a read
of the low 128 bits, as `read_simd` does, and a `word_insert` of the new
low half into the 512-bit element. VZEROUPPER clears bits 128 to 511 of
ZMM0 to ZMM15 in Sail; s2n-bignum writes each `YMMi` with the
zero-extended low half, and `zerotop_256` clears bits 256 to 511 as well,
so they agree.

The checks of `check_legacy_simd` (is the instruction legal with this
prefix, does the processor have SSE4.1 or AES-NI) run before the selected
bodies and are not compared; neither are `vex_feature_present` for the
VEX forms and the alignment checks of the memory forms.

PINSRQ (66 REX.W 0F 3A 22) is the same opcode as PINSRD with REX.W set.
Its body chooses the quadword with `match sel` on `unsigned(imm[0])`,
which the translator expands into a conditional
([backend README](../backend/README.md#what-the-translator-checks-or-refuses),
"Integer matches"), as for VPINSRQ. Not done in this suite: PINSRB,
PINSRW and every form with a memory operand.

PCLMULQDQ selects the two quadwords with imm8[0] and imm8[4], as
VPCLMULQDQ does, and uses the same unrolled carry-less multiplication
proof. PSLLDQ and PSRLDQ shift the whole 128-bit value by imm8 bytes,
not independently within smaller elements. Their statements keep all
256 count values, including counts at or above 16, which produce zero.

### Stack

The `x86-stack` suite compares LEAVE (C9) in long mode without an
operand-size override. The entry retains `decode_execute`'s C9 branch,
`opsize_default64`, `stack_addr_size`, `get_seg_base` and `pop`; it
supplies only the acquired opcode and the default prefix record.
The original body sets RSP to RBP, loads the saved frame pointer,
increments RSP by eight and writes RBP.

`read_mem_data(64, address)` is a declared interpretation: read eight
little-endian bytes of total `memory` and prepend one `EventLoad`.
The theorem compares complete states at every 64-bit frame pointer,
including wrapping RSP arithmetic, unchanged memory and the original
event trace. It does not compare paging, permissions, faults, data
breakpoints, instruction fetch or RIP advancement. The load event
comes from this interpretation, not from Sail's event semantics.
See [interpretations](../backend/README.md#interpretations).

REMS also has a LEAVE body, but it is outside the original GPR/VECTOR
comparison scope. This addition does not extend REMS's memory interface
or claim a REMS LEAVE theorem.

### AES-NI

The `x86-aes` suite compares AESENC, AESENCLAST, AESDEC, AESDECLAST and
AESKEYGENASSIST (`66 0F 38 DC` to `DF`, `66 0F 3A DF`) in register form.
The entries are those of the SSE suite: a legacy prefix record, the
opcode of `dispatch_3byte_38_sse_int` or `dispatch_3byte_3a_sse_int`
fixed, and the ModR/M byte (and the round constant of AESKEYGENASSIST)
supplied. The destination keeps bits 128 to 511, so the statements use
`SIMD128_SSE`. The AES-NI availability check of `check_legacy_simd` is
outside the selected bodies and is not compared.

shina computes the S-box by field arithmetic: `aes_sbox` is the affine
map of `gf2p8_inv`, which is a chain of eleven `gf2p8_mul` calls of eight
unrolled steps each, and MixColumns multiplies by the constants 2 and 3
(9, 11, 13 and 14 for the inverse) with the same `gf2p8_mul`.
s2n-bignum's `aesenc` and its relatives look each byte up in the tables
of `common/aes.ml`. The proof relates the two without supplying either:

- For each of the two S-boxes and the six multiplications, the generated
  function equals the table lookup for every input byte. Each of these
  eight lemmas is a proof by 256 cases; a case evaluates the generated
  definition on a numeral (`x86-aes-lemmas.ml` substitutes a `let` only
  after its value is a numeral, since copying the expression would make
  the product chain exponentially large) and the table lookup on the
  other side. They take most of the ten minutes that the suite runs.
- SubBytes, ShiftRows, MixColumns and the inverses of the three then
  differ from s2n-bignum's only in how the sixteen bytes are joined. The
  table lookups are opaque there, and bit-blasting compares the packing.
- Each instruction relation is compared after these rewrites. The
  statements use the unexpanded root definitions, so that the generated
  AES functions are rewritten before they are unfolded.

## REMS

REMS's instruction bodies take the decoded instruction as arguments:
processor mode, start and current instruction pointer, the prefix
record, the REX byte, the opcode and the ModR/M and SIB bytes. Each entry
calls one body with a register-direct encoding. ADD64, for example, is

```text
x86_add_adc_sub_sbb_or_and_xor_cmp_g_e(0, Mode_64bit, 0, 3,
  Mk_prefixes(...), 0b0100 @ 0b1 @ d[3..3] @ 0b0 @ m[3..3],
  0x03, Mk_modr_m(0b11 @ d[2..0] @ m[2..0]), Mk_sib(0x00))
```

that is `ADD r64, r/m64` (REX.W 03 /r) with destination `d` and source
`m`. The body keeps its operand decoding, arithmetic, flags and RIP
update. The synthetic span from 0 to the end of the ModR/M byte passes
the body's length and canonical-address checks; it does not fix the HOL
RIP. The shift count byte replaces the body's immediate fetch.

Within the original 205-form comparison scope, REMS has no body for the
vector forms or for ADCX, ADOX and MULX at its pinned commit. Every
branch of its VEX dispatch
(`model/vex_opcodes_dispatch.sail`) ends in `x86_model_error("Opcode
Unimplemented in x86isa!")` or a fault, and its `ext_vex_*` hooks return
false; MULX is VEX opcode 0F 38 F6. ADCX and ADOX are in the non-VEX
0F 38 map, whose escape throws "Translation error: two-byte opcode 56
not translated" (`model/two_byte_opcodes_dispatch.sail`). These forms
are compared for shina only. The shina forms added after that scope were
not inventoried or exported for REMS, so they are not classified as
missing REMS bodies.

REMS bodies reach Sail constructs that the Arm, RV32 and shina exports do
not. The REMS profile (`extended_evaluation` and the
`Uncaught_exceptions` binding in [x86_rems.ml](../backend/x86_rems.ml))
turns on these translator rules, each part of the trusted translation:

- A throw (`x86_fault`, `x86_model_error`) permits no final state; the
  translator supports no `try`. Since s2n-bignum's relations always have
  a final state, an `_EQ` theorem shows every throw unreachable for the
  inputs it covers.
- A shift by an amount that the translator cannot prove nonnegative
  becomes `if &0 <= amount then <shift> else u`, with `u` an unknown word
  (`Sail-negative-shift-unknown`). REMS's 8- and 16-bit rotates shift by
  `width - count`, which is negative for counts above the rotated width.
- An `undefined` value of a refined integer type, such as `nat`, is a
  choice within the refinement; a choice outside it permits no final state
  (`Sail-undefined-refined-integer`).
- Direct recursion on integer values, such as `logcount_int`, is
  unfolded while one argument shrinks and none grows, up to fixed limits.
  Each unfolded call is the original body.
- The profile also decides more conditions statically and supports
  matches on Booleans, guarded variables and short-circuit values with
  one continuation. These translate the same relation; the other sources
  keep the earlier translation so that their models stay unchanged.

REMS's GPR accessors `read_gpr` and `write_gpr` are cuts. Before cutting
them the translator checks that they use the x86 register numbering (0
RAX, 1 RCX, ..., 15 R15) and that `write_gpr` does nothing but its update
and `log_gpr_write`. REMS's 32-bit `rflags` and its `rip` are root
parameters, which the statements instantiate as `rflags :> bottom_32` and
`RIP`. Each flag in REMS's undefined-flag mask gets an independent
unknown value.

REMS bodies end by writing the next instruction address to RIP; here
that is the end of the synthetic span, a constant. s2n-bignum's relations
leave RIP unchanged. The statements therefore compare

```text
sail_hl_x86_core R s t <=> ?u. R s u /\ t = write RIP (read RIP s) u
```

which resets RIP to its entry value and observes every other component.
REMS roots, types and constructors carry the output stem in their names
(`sail_hl_X86_5fREMS_5f...`, `..._x86_2drems_2d...`), so both models can
load into one kernel.

## What the proofs show

For shina, every arithmetic, vector, move, select and byte-swap form agrees
with s2n-bignum, including all register aliases, every flag and the rest
of the state; BT, BTS, BTR and BTC are the exception described next.
Every shift and rotate agrees except RCL and RCR at 8 and 16 bits. For
those, an `_EXACT` theorem states shina's relation exactly: s2n-bignum's
relation with OF also kept at whole-cycle counts. The four bit-test
instructions have the same pair of theorems, with ZF kept at every
operand. So shina permits a
subset of what s2n-bignum permits, and a `_DIFF` theorem shows that the
inclusion is strict.

For REMS, every arithmetic form agrees. Among the shifts and rotates, SHL
and SHR at every width, SAR, RCL and RCR at 32 and 64 bits, and SHLD and
SHRD at every width agree. The other REMS forms (ROL and ROR at every
width; SAR, RCL and RCR at 8 and 16 bits) have `_DIFF` theorems, which
show that at least these differences exist:

- ROR's carry flag;
- 8- and 16-bit SAR's carry flag at counts of at least the width;
- ROL's and ROR's OF at masked count zero;
- 8- and 16-bit rotates at counts above the rotated width, where the
  negative-shift rule above gives an unknown result.

No exact relation is proved for these forms.
[X86-DIVERGENCES.md](X86-DIVERGENCES.md) gives each difference, its
witness and which relation permits more.

## Shift corrections

s2n-bignum's narrow shift relations used to fix values that the
architecture leaves undefined. The comparison with shina showed this,
and awslabs/s2n-bignum#484 corrects it. The corrected relations
keep the undefined choices and preserve every unaffected state
component.

- 8- and 16-bit SHL and SHR: Sail's `exec_shl` and `exec_shr` make CF
  undefined when the count is at least the operand width
  (`model/insn_groups.sail:297,334`). The old `x86_SHL` and `x86_SHR`
  always set CF to the last bit shifted out; for an 8-bit zero operand and
  count 8 that fixed CF to false. The masked counts concerned are 8 to 31
  for bytes and 16 to 31 for 16-bit operands; 32- and 64-bit counts are
  masked below the width.
- 16-bit SHLD and SHRD: for counts above the width, Sail's `exec_shld`
  and `exec_shrd` make the result and all six flags undefined
  (`model/insn_baseline.sail:365-373,424-432`). The old relations fixed
  the result and CF, SF, ZF and PF. With AX = BX = 0, `SHLD AX,BX,17` now
  permits AX = 1 as well as 0.

Intel's Software Developer's Manual, Volume 2B (order 253667-093US),
specifies undefined values in both cases (pages 4-606 and 4-640 to
4-644). The proofs in [x86-shift.ml](../proofs/x86-shift.ml) cover every
count byte for these forms. Neither correction changes decodable
behaviour of the library: no 8- or 16-bit SHL or SHR occurs in it, and
the decoder does not accept 16-bit SHLD or SHRD.
